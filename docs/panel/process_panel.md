# WISE Workbench — Process Panel Report

Joint report of a three-person panel: a process-mining researcher/practitioner (**PM**), a BPM expert (**BPM**) and a Lean / Six Sigma Master Black Belt (**BB**). Where the panel disagreed, the split and its resolution are marked **Panel split**. Library references are to `wise` (`wise-pm`), paper references to Jessen, Fahland, Zerbato, *WISE: Actionable Norm-Based Scoring for Process Mining*.

## 0. Panel position

1. WISE is a *decision layer*, not a discovery tool. The workbench must be organised around the improvement loop (question → norm → backlog → validation → hypothesis → intervention → monitoring), not around the event log. The log is an input; the versioned norm, the backlog snapshot, the validation record and the action card are the products.
2. The method "moves judgement to the right place" (paper Sec. V-D). The app automates computation and bookkeeping around human decisions (case notion, applicability, thresholds, weights, γ, validation reading, root cause, action) and never makes those decisions itself. This is the design rule that settles most later questions.
3. Local-first is a trust feature: logs, norms and LLM traffic stay on the machine; every artefact is a plain, diffable file (norm JSON, run manifest JSON, backlog CSV/Parquet, cards as JSON/Markdown). A project must be reproducible from its folder without the app.
4. One norm, several views, one backlog per (norm version, view, slice key, period). The UI must always show which of these four the user is looking at.

**Where WISE computes and where judgement is indispensable.** Library outputs carry S1 (validation diagnostics), S5–S7 and S11 almost completely, and support S3 (distributions), S6 (drivers) and S8 (drill-down). Judgement cannot be delegated at S0, S2 (case notion is the single most consequential choice; the paper's PO-item vs PO-header distinction is a design decision, not a computation), S3 thresholds, S4 weights, S6 dispositions, S7 readings, S8 causes, S9 owners and S11 re-baselining. **Panel split:** BPM wanted a formal approval workflow for norm versions in MVP; PM objected to workflow machinery before the method is trusted. **Resolution:** MVP records status (draft / reviewed / approved) with name, date and comment on each norm version, no routing engine; approval gates exports labelled "for governance".

### 3.1 Norm-elicitation UX: "invoices should be paid within terms"

1. **Kind of expectation** (one question, five answers): something must happen / must not happen / order / time between two things / how often / values must agree → picks `Presence` / `Exclusion` / `Precedence` / `Lag` / `Singularity` / `Balance` / `Metric`. Here: time between.
2. **Endpoints**: fuzzy match "invoice" and "paid" against the activity inventory with counts; synonyms become one merged label list (`["Record Invoice Receipt", "Vendor creates invoice"]` → a). Unknown labels cannot be saved.
3. **Scope**: which cases does this apply to? Matrix over flow type / document type with counts; "not consignment" becomes an applicability rule.
4. **Threshold**: "within terms" is per case, so the wizard offers two encodings and shows both: fixed δ (e.g. 30 days) with width Δ, or a derived attribute `days_late = lag(a,b) − payment_terms` scored by `Metric(threshold=0, width=…)`. The calibrator (F8) shows the ECDF, the share violated at the chosen δ/Δ, and the mean ν.
5. **Meaning**: layer (existing or new), within-layer weight, one-sentence rationale, owner name. The wizard writes the sentence back: "For DF1/DF2/2-way items, Clear Invoice should follow invoice receipt within 30 days; violation saturates at 90 days."
6. **Preview**: score the sample; show which existing constraints are correlated (> 0.8) to avoid double counting; save as a new norm version with changelog.

**Panel split:** PM wanted the LLM to propose thresholds from the distribution; BB and BPM refused numbers from a model in a stakeholder artefact. **Resolution:** the LLM may draft steps 1–3 and 5 (type, endpoints, scope, sentence) and may *describe* the distribution; step 4 is always a human drag on the calibrator.

## 6. Trust and adoption

**What makes a ranked backlog trusted.** (a) Reproducibility: same log fingerprint + norm fingerprint + parameters → identical backlog, verifiable by the auditor. (b) Traceability: every slice opens to its cases and every case to the events that triggered each ν. (c) Transparency of the formula per row (n, μ̄, μ_s, γ, gap, PI) in the table, not in a manual. (d) Visible support and stability: n, `PI_lower`, sensitivity badge. (e) Face validity: owners recognise their top-3 or can dispute it, and the dispute is stored. (f) The view is always named; a backlog without its view is not shown. (g) Stable PI is primary, raw PI visible beside it, γ printed (**Panel split** PM vs BB on which to headline; resolution as stated, because owners must see that stabilisation is a choice).

**What must be auditable.** Norm versions with diff, status, approver and date; derived-attribute recipes; column mapping and log fingerprint; run manifest (norm and log fingerprints, library version, view, slice keys, γ, `min_cases`, baseline, volume mode, exclusions); validation readings and overrides; hotspot-label overrides; dispositions from review sessions; action cards with evidence links; every LLM suggestion that was accepted (model, prompt hash, output, who accepted).

**Presenting uncertainty and data-quality caveats.** The data-readiness report is a banner on every screen and page of the governance pack, not a hidden tab. Each slice row carries: support (n), `PI_lower` at a stated z, gap retained without censored cases, replicated share, sensitivity class (stable / shifts / fragile) from F22. Unscored cases and applicability density are reported next to the score distribution. Language rules: "priority" and "expectation shortfall", never "root cause", "fault" or "effect" in generated text; value constraints are labelled commercial-risk indicators, not audit findings (paper Sec. V-C).

**What not to automate.** Setting thresholds, widths, weights or γ; approving a norm version; assigning owners; excluding cases; declaring a root cause; declaring an intervention successful; re-baselining; sending reports to people. All of these are one-click actions for a human with a mandatory note.

**LLM assistant (local Ollama) per stage.** Rules that hold everywhere: suggestions arrive as diffs or cards, never as applied changes; every generated sentence that contains a number must cite the table it came from (the app injects the tables, the model may not compute); the model never sees more than the aggregate tables and the sampled traces the user has opened; the model is off by default in the owner portal.

| Stage | Should | Should not |
|---|---|---|
| S1–S2 | Explain the readiness report in plain words; propose a column mapping and a flow-type rule for confirmation | Edit data; decide the case notion |
| S3 | Draft type, endpoints, scope and the rationale sentence from an expectation; ask clarifying questions; propose a layer; point out overlaps | Propose or write thresholds, widths or weights; invent activity labels not in the inventory |
| S4 | Turn a stated goal into a draft layer emphasis for discussion | Claim a weight set is correct or "typical" |
| S6 | Narrate a slice for its owner: type, dominant layer, top constraints, validation status, in owner vocabulary | Speculate on causes; rank owners by blame |
| S7 | Explain censoring and replication and suggest which source table to check | Set the reading |
| S8 | Draft fishbone bones and 5-why questions from drivers; summarise opened traces | Assert a cause; extrapolate from a handful of traces |
| S9–S10 | Draft card text from linked evidence; check card completeness | Choose the owner or the countermeasure |
| S11 | Summarise period changes against the baseline with the caveats | Use causal language ("the intervention reduced…") |
| S12 | Draft the method appendix and the norm's plain-language documentation | Change the norm |

## 7. Success metrics for the app and top-10 risks

**Success metrics (measured by the app itself, per project).** Time from log import to first approved norm (target < 10 working days). Share of constraints with an owner-calibrated threshold (target 100 % before "approved"). Number of backlog-review sessions held with owners and share of top-k slices dispositioned. Share of focus slices that passed the validation gate before a card existed (target 100 %). Cards with owner, evidence and expected effect (target: no card without them). Time from new extract to period report (target < 2 days). Rank stability of the top-10 across the sensitivity sweep. Owner agreement rate on the top-10 (agree / dispute). LLM suggestion acceptance and edit rates, kept per stage. Reproducibility checks passed by the auditor. Norms reused across ≥ 2 periods and ≥ 2 owners; second cycle started without the original analyst.

**Top-10 risks and mitigations.**
1. Norm engineering is too heavy and the workshop stalls → wizard, templates, "10 constraints first" default, per-constraint save.
2. Wrong case notion or flow-type rule silently produces a wrong backlog → S2 gate, coverage counts, sensitivity to case notion shown in the readiness report.
3. Replicated or bulk events inflate rework signals → replication diagnostics in the banner, gate at S7, `cross_case_replication` per slice.
4. False precision: owners treat PI as truth → formula popover, `PI_lower`, stability badges, language rules.
5. LLM invents activities, causes or numbers → inventory-bound labels, table-cited numbers, accept-only workflow, provenance log.
6. Thresholds set by the analyst, not the owner → calibration note with owner name required for "approved".
