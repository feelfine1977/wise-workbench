# Analytics layer for the WISE Workbench — ML specialist report

**Scope.** Which analytics belong on top of `wise` (ScoreResult, backlogs, diagnostics) in a local-first app with an Ollama assistant, and how to build them so that every number is explainable, reproducible and auditable on a laptop.

**Starting point.** `wise` already delivers the hard part *exactly*: bounded violations `V`, view scores `S`, the exact decomposition `1 − S = Σ_λ Δ_λ = Σ_c π_c` with `π_c(σ) = w̃_c,σ ν_c(σ)` (`ScoreResult.penalties`), `PI = v_s·(μ̄ − μ_s)_+` with shrinkage `γ`, drivers, penalty mass, hotspot typology, censoring/replication diagnostics. The analytics layer must not re-model what is exact. Its jobs are: (a) say how sure we are of what WISE says, (b) contrast and characterise hotspots on WISE's own artefacts, (c) help engineer and validate the norm and the data, (d) watch the same backlog over time, (e) compute honest headroom *under the norm*. Nothing below estimates causal effects; the paper (Sec. VI-B) is explicit that WISE yields action hypotheses, and the app must keep that boundary in every sentence it renders. The earlier prototype's SHAP/random-forest/isolation-forest layer is not carried over (Sec. 2).

Notation (paper Sec. IV): `μ_s, μ̄` slice and global mean score; `gap_s = (μ̄ − μ_s)_+`; `μ̃_s = n_s/(n_s+γ)·μ_s + γ/(n_s+γ)·μ̄`; `v_s ∈ {n_s, E_s}`; `π_c`, `Δ_λ` as above; `r = Σ \ Σ_s` the rest of the log.

## 1. Analytics that add value, ordered by value/effort

Ordering criterion: value to the analyst's next decision divided by implementation and run-time cost, with a penalty for any method that needs a fitted model. Items 1.1–1.3 and the headroom part of 1.5 are the MVP.

### 1.1 Uncertainty of PI and rank stability (value high, effort low — MVP)
- **Question.** Is slice `s` really in the top-k, or does its priority hinge on a few cases, on `γ`, or on a threshold choice? (Paper Sec. VI-C asks for exactly this.)
- **Input.** `ScoreResult.frame(view)` (score + slice keys + exposure), backlog `attrs` (γ, baseline, volume); `EventLog` + `Norm` for threshold perturbation.
- **Method.** (i) *Case bootstrap by weights*: draw `w^(b) ~ Multinomial(n, 1/n)` (or per-cluster when cases nest in documents: `cluster="purchasing_document"`), compute `μ_s^(b) = Σ_{σ∈s} w_σ S_σ / Σ_{σ∈s} w_σ` by weighted groupby, `μ̄^(b)` likewise (or fixed baseline), then `PĨ_s^(b)` and ranks; B = 200–500, chunks of 50 replicates (50 × 251 k float32 = 50 MB). Report `q05/q95` of `stable_gap` and `stable_PI`, rank interval, `P(rank ≤ k) = B⁻¹ Σ_b 1[rank_s^(b) ≤ k]`. (ii) *Sensitivity envelope*: γ grid `{0, γ/2, γ, 2γ, γ̂_MoM}` (`estimate_gamma`); per graded constraint scale `(δ,Δ)`, `(τ,Γ)`, `(k,K)` by `{0.8, 1.25}`; view weights jittered ±10 % and renormalised. Threshold changes do not alter applicability, so `S' = 1 − Σ_c w̃_c V'_c` is recomputed from the cached `V` with one column re-evaluated (`evaluate_constraint`) — milliseconds, no full rescoring. Metrics: `top_k_overlap` (Jaccard), Kendall τ_b on the top-K union, per-slice rank range.
- **Output.** Per slice: point PI, 90 % interval, rank interval, `P(top-k)`, badge *stable* (`P(top-k) ≥ 0.8` and in top-k under every perturbation) / *fragile* / *insufficient support* (`n_s < 10`). Shown as dot-and-interval strip beside the backlog and as the volume–gap scatter of paper Fig. 6 with whiskers.
- **Validation.** Interval coverage on synthetic logs ≥ 85 % at nominal 90 %; bootstrap of an unperturbed synthetic null gives `P(top-k)` ≈ k/|S|; BPIC'19 focus slices (Packaging, Logistics, Real Estate) must come out *stable*; deterministic under a fixed seed.
- **Failure modes.** Tiny slices give degenerate intervals (badge, never "stable"); dependence between cases of one document or replicated events breaks the i.i.d. bootstrap (cluster bootstrap, and 1.3 must run first); recomputed vs fixed baseline gives different intervals (state which); the perturbation envelope is not a probability distribution — never quote it as one.

### 1.2 Contrastive gap decomposition: hotspot vs rest, with effect sizes (value high, effort low — MVP)
- **Question.** What carries the gap of slice `s`: which constraints and layers, how large in raw units (days, %, counts), and is it a shift of the whole slice or a long tail?
- **Input.** `penalties(view)`, `violations`, `in_scope`, `cases`; raw signals per graded constraint (Sec. 3, `raw_signals`).
- **Method.** Exact identity, no model: `μ̄ − μ_s = Σ_c [mean_s(π_c) − mean_Σ(π_c)] = Σ_λ [mean_s(Δ_λ) − mean_Σ(Δ_λ)]` (this is `layer_drivers`' `__delta`, extended to constraints), and against the rest `mean_s − mean_r = (n/n_r)·(mean_s − mean_Σ)`. Per constraint add: violation-rate difference `RD = P_s(ν>0) − P_r(ν>0)` with Newcombe CI and relative risk; share evaluated in `s` vs `r` (separates "violates more" from "more constraints apply" — under `layer_balanced` weights the applicable set changes `w̃`); for graded constraints the raw shift: medians, Hodges–Lehmann shift in native units, Cliff's δ, and the ECDF overlay of paper Fig. 7. Shift-vs-tail reading: compare `Q50` and `Q90` shifts — similar ⇒ whole-distribution shift (queue/workload), only `Q90` ⇒ tail (triage). CIs reuse the bootstrap weights of 1.1.
- **Output.** Waterfall from `μ̄` to `μ_s` by constraint (bars sum exactly to the gap) with CI whiskers; ECDF overlay for the top graded driver; one reading per driver ("GR→INV lag: median 83 d in slice vs 55 d elsewhere; whole distribution shifted").
- **Validation.** Property test `|Σ_c delta_c − gap| < 1e-9`; synthetic planted hotspot: top driver equals the planted constraint in ≥ 95 % of runs; shift/tail reading matches the planted mechanism; BPIC'19 golden values (Packaging IR→CI median 83 vs 55 d).
- **Failure modes.** Unscored/unevaluated cases drop silently (always print `n_scored`, `share_evaluated`); flow-type mixture inside the slice (show composition, offer within-flow contrast); time confounding — a slice concentrated in late months looks censored (show case-start histogram); tiny reference groups; and wording: "carries", "is associated with", never "because".

### 1.3 Data-quality and drift checks — the readiness gate (value high, effort low — MVP)
- **Question.** Can this backlog be acted on, or is the signal a window, logging or extraction artefact?
- **Input.** `EventLog`, `ScoreResult`, backlog; existing `EventLog.validate`, `timestamp_outliers`, `right_censored`, `left_truncated`, `event_replication`, `cross_case_replication`, `gap_retained`, `validation_table`.
- **Method (additions, robust descriptive statistics only).** Sentinel dates (any single timestamp carrying > 1 % of events, dates outside the robust window); exact duplicate events (case, activity, timestamp, attributes); timestamp-precision mix per activity (date-only vs datetime — ties inflate replication and zero lags); activity-vocabulary drift by period: frequency vector `f_p`, Jensen–Shannon divergence to the reference period, new/vanished labels, `Norm.check` per period; attribute-cardinality drift (new vendors share); exposure sanity (order-of-magnitude histogram per company to catch unit mixing); censoring impact on the top-k via `gap_retained`; window-edge share: fraction of a slice's cases starting within `δ+Δ` of the window end for its dominant lag constraint.
- **Output.** Readiness panel: each check pass/warn/fail with the number; per top-k slice the paper's Table XII reading. Slices with `retained < 0.5` or replicated share ≥ 0.5 carry a banner, and the assistant is blocked from stating action hypotheses for them (Sec. 4).
- **Validation.** Planted artefacts (replicated header events, sentinel dates, truncated window) recovered with recall ≥ 0.95 on synthetic logs; BPIC'19 golden values (Logistics replicated share 72.5 %, 0 % censoring in focus slices).
- **Failure modes.** A genuine system change reads as drift (needs analyst confirmation → new norm version); thresholds (1 %, 0.5) are conventions — configurable and recorded; drift metrics on short periods are noisy (minimum events per period).

### 1.4 Norm-engineering aids (value high for onboarding, effort low–medium — v1)
- **Question.** What thresholds and widths does the empirical distribution suggest, where is a constraint meaningless (applicability), which constraints are dead, saturated or redundant?
- **Input.** Raw signals per constraint from the same log primitives as scoring; `violations`, `in_scope`; a reference population chosen by the analyst (all cases, a "good" slice, or a period).
- **Method.** (a) *Threshold proposal*: ECDF of the raw signal on the reference; propose `δ = Q_{q1}` (default 0.75), `Δ = Q_{q2} − δ` (0.95), for singularity `k = Q_0.9` of counts and `K` so that `Q_0.99` saturates, for balance `τ = Q_0.75(d)`; show resulting share violated and mean ν; bootstrap the quantiles. Labelled *as-is calibration*; SLA/policy values override. (b) *Applicability proposal*: for constraint `c` with activities `A` and candidate attribute `β`, presence rate `p_v = P(cnt(A,σ) > 0 | att_β(σ) = v)`; if `p_v < ε` (2 %) propose "not applicable for v" with the evidence sentence ("GR occurs in 0.4 % of 2-way items"). (c) *Constraint health*: share violated < 0.5 % (dead) or > 95 % (saturated: mis-specified or a data problem), share evaluated, pairwise `φ` on `1[ν>0]` over jointly evaluated cases (redundancy), with *structurally implied* pairs derived from the definitions (Presence(a) violated ⇒ Lag(a,·, missing_a=violate) violated) flagged rather than reported as insight. (d) *View diagnostics*: realised mean `w̃` per layer vs intended `a_λ` (applicability shifts effective emphasis), `view_agreement`.
- **Output.** Proposals appear in the norm editor as diffs with evidence; nothing is applied without acceptance; acceptance changes the norm fingerprint.
- **Validation.** Synthetic logs with known generating distributions: proposed `δ` recovers the generator quantile within sampling error; planted flow-type scope recovered by (b); health flags on the BPIC'19 norm reviewed once and frozen as a golden list.
- **Failure modes.** Circularity — calibrating the norm to the as-is turns "expected" into "usual" and gaps vanish (hence explicit reference population, label, sign-off); heavy tails make quantiles unstable; overlap ≠ error (intentional double coverage in a layer).

### 1.5 What-if and headroom under the norm (headroom MVP, norm what-if v1)
- **Question.** (a) How much of a slice's PI is attributable to constraint `c` — what if `c` were fully satisfied there? (b) How does the backlog change under another norm, view weighting or `γ`? (c) What if slice `s` behaved like the reference on driver `c`?
- **Input.** `ScoreResult`, backlog `attrs`; (b) a modified `Norm` (`Norm.replace_constraint`, `View`).
- **Method.** (a) Headroom `H_s(c) = v_s · mean_s(π_c)`. With the baseline held fixed, setting `ν_c = 0` in `s` gives `PI'_s = v_s·(μ̄ − μ_s − mean_s(π_c))_+`, so the reduction is exactly `min(PI_s, H_s(c))` (times `n_s/(n_s+γ)` for the stabilised index). Exact, additive over `c`. (b) `score(log, norm2)` (≈2 s on BPIC'19), then `top_k_overlap`, rank shifts, `Δμ̄`, applicability density before/after; weight- or γ-only what-ifs reuse cached `V` (`S' = 1 − Σ_c w̃'_c V_c`). (c) Replace `ν_c` of slice cases by quantile-mapped values from the reference distribution of `ν_c`, recompute `S`, PI with fixed baseline.
- **Output.** Headroom bars per driver ("fully meeting GR→INV timeliness would remove at most 61 % of this slice's PI *under the norm*"); what-if diff table (rank/gap before → after, top-k Jaccard). Always the phrase "under the norm, if …", never "expected benefit".
- **Validation.** Identities: unchanged norm reproduces the backlog bit-for-bit; `Σ_c H_s(c) = v_s·(1 − μ_s)`; headroom ≤ PI; removing every constraint gives PI = 0; synthetic: removing the planted driver removes the planted gap.
- **Failure modes.** Headroom read as a prediction (interventions interact, applicability shifts, `μ̄` moves — we hold it fixed and say so); what-ifs on unevaluable constraints; norm what-ifs that silently change scope.

### 1.6 Period-over-period monitoring and SPC-style limits (value high once runs repeat, effort medium — v1)
- **Question.** Did a slice's gap or a constraint's violation rate change over time or after an intervention, beyond noise?
- **Input.** Per-period backlogs with a common fixed baseline (`prioritize(baseline=…)`, `compare_periods`), per-period constraint rates; period key = case-start month/quarter as a derived attribute; norm fingerprint pinned across periods.
- **Method.** Series `x_{s,t} = μ_{s,t}` or `p_{c,s,t} = P(ν_c > 0)`. Phase I (first `T_0 ≥ 3` periods) sets `μ_0`, pooled within-slice `σ_0`. X̄-chart limits `μ_0 ± 3σ_0/√n_{s,t}`; p-chart `p̄ ± 3√(p̄(1−p̄)/n_{s,t})`; CUSUM `C⁺_t = max(0, C⁺_{t−1} + (x_t − μ_0)/σ_{s,t} − 0.5)` with `σ_{s,t} = σ_0/√n_{s,t}`, signal at `C⁺ ≥ 4–5` for sustained small shifts; magnitude reported in raw units (median lag per period). Censoring control: cases started within `δ+Δ` of the window end are excluded from the last period or scored with `missing_b="censor"`, so the latest point is not spuriously worse. Optional same-period-last-year reference.
- **Output.** Small-multiple control charts for the top-k slices and their dominant constraints; a change table (slice, metric, since period, direction, magnitude, confirmed = 2 consecutive signals) ranked by `n·|shift|`, plus the expected number of false alarms for the number of charts shown.
- **Validation.** Synthetic planted step at `t*`: detection delay ≤ 2 periods for a 1σ shift, false-alarm rate ≤ 1 per 100 slice-periods under the null; BPIC'19 quarterly series as regression fixture.
- **Failure modes.** Many charts ⇒ chance alarms (show expected count; require confirmation); volume changes move limits; norm edits between periods break comparability (block unless fingerprints match); regression to the mean after acting on a slice chosen for its extreme (shrinkage, ≥ 3 pre-periods); seasonality.

### 1.7 Violation signatures and variant severity (value medium, effort low — v1)
- **Question.** Which combinations of violations recur, and which control-flow variants carry the penalty?
- **Input.** `1[V > 0]` (constraint or layer level), `penalties`, `log` traces.
- **Method.** Signature = binary pattern over constraints (or over the layers: at most `2^|Λ|`, 128 for the BPIC'19 norm); group cases, rank by penalty mass, Pareto (top-N + "other"), prototype case via `worst_cases` within the signature, mean raw signals; variant severity = activity sequence/multiset × `n` × mean score × penalty mass. Clustering only when > 200 signatures cover < 80 % of the mass — then k-medoids with Jaccard distance on the top signatures (scipy). Structural implications from 1.4 label signatures such as "missing INV ⇒ missing CLR ⇒ lag undefined" as *by construction*.
- **Output.** Signature table with reading and prototype; variant Pareto with scores. **Validation.** `Σ_signature mass = Σ(1 − S)`; planted syndrome is the top signature. **Failure modes.** Combinatorial explosion (cap at layer level or top-N); variants unstable under replicated events; signatures mistaken for mechanisms.

### 1.8 PI-guided subgroup discovery instead of tree surrogates (value medium, effort medium — v1)
- **Question.** Are there attribute conjunctions beyond the chosen slice keys that concentrate underperformance?
- **Input.** `frame(view)` with ≤ 10 case attributes, cardinality capped (top-30 values + other).
- **Method.** Beam search (width 20, depth ≤ 3) over conjunctions with quality `q(s) = n_s · n_s/(n_s+γ) · (μ̄ − μ_s)_+` — the stabilised PI itself, so discovered subgroups are commensurable with the backlog; minimum support 100; *discover on a random half, confirm on the other half* and report only the confirmed gap; drop subgroups nested in a better one. `sklearn.tree.DecisionTreeRegressor(max_depth=3, min_samples_leaf≥max(50, 1 % n))` on `1 − S` is an optional alternative whose leaves are candidate slices — never a "predictor".
- **Output.** Candidate slices with `n`, confirmed gap, PI, interval (1.1), the conjunction in words; one click turns a subgroup into a derived attribute (`eval` recipe) in the norm, after which every other analytic applies.
- **Validation.** Planted conjunction recovered (precision/recall over runs); on null logs a confirmed subgroup exceeding the top-20 PI cutoff appears in ≤ 5 % of runs. **Failure modes.** Selection bias (holdout mitigates, does not remove), high-cardinality attributes, subgroups that are flow-type mixtures (report composition), causal reading.

### 1.9 Resource and hand-off analysis (value conditional on a resource column, effort medium — later)
- **Question.** Where do manual touches and hand-offs concentrate, and do they coincide with penalty? **Input.** Events with resource; recipes (`count_events` regex, `nunique` resources; new recipe kind `handoffs` = number of resource changes along the trace); `penalties`. **Method.** `penalty_mass` by resource/role; hand-off matrix `r_i → r_j` with counts and mean downstream penalty; lag distribution per pair; segregation-of-duties only with a supplied role table. **Output.** Pareto by resource, hand-off heatmap, descriptive only. **Validation.** Identity checks; planted slow pair. **Failure modes.** Batch/system users, anonymised resources, resource ≠ owner, blame framing (present as workload, not performance).

### 1.10 Open-case exceedance risk, survival-style (value medium, effort medium — later)
- **Question.** Which open cases (`a` observed, `b` not yet) will miss the lag norm, and how many new violations to expect per slice?
- **Input.** Log at an as-of date, one `Lag(a, b, δ, Δ)`, slice keys.
- **Method.** Mature cohort = cases with `t_a ≤ as_of − H`, `H = max(δ+Δ, Q_0.99 lag)`, fully observed; empirical survival `Ŝ(t)` over the cohort with never-`b` cases as lag = ∞; for an open case of age `t`: `P(miss δ) = Ŝ(δ)/Ŝ(t)`, `P(saturate) = Ŝ(δ+Δ)/Ŝ(t)`; stratify by flow type/slice when the stratum has ≥ 200 mature cases, else pool; Kaplan–Meier (numpy, ~30 lines) only when no mature cohort exists. Expected new violations per slice `= Σ_open P(miss)`.
- **Output.** Triage list (case, age, `P(miss)`, exposure) and expected violations per slice, labelled as a base-rate extrapolation. **Validation.** Backtests at earlier as-of dates: calibration curve, Brier score vs base rate; identity `P = 1` for `t > δ`. **Failure modes.** Immortal cases (never-`b` mass shown separately); regime change makes the cohort stale; informative censoring; double counting with `missing_b="censor"` scoring (use one or the other).

## 2. What not to do, and why
- **Black-box predictors of low scores presented as root causes.** `S` is a deterministic function of the constraints; a random forest predicting `S` from attributes reconstructs the norm plus data availability, and its feature importance reflects weights and applicability, not causes. Allowed only as the optional slice-finder of 1.8, phrased as "candidate slices".
- **SHAP/LIME on scores.** `1 − S = Σ_c π_c` is already the exact additive attribution: for a function that is linear in the `ν_c` with known weights, the Shapley value of `c` against the population baseline is `π_c(σ) − mean_Σ(π_c)`, which is precisely the contrast of 1.2. SHAP would add sampling noise to an exact quantity and cost minutes on a laptop; attribute-level attribution is the exact groupby contrast of 1.2.
- **Unsupervised anomaly detection (isolation forests, autoencoders) on `V` or `S` as "deviation" evidence.** It reintroduces the implicit analyst lens the paper argues against; anomalies are defined by the norm. Anomaly detection is confined to data quality (timestamps, duplicates, vocabulary) in 1.3.
- **Causal claims from observational logs.** "Vendor X causes delays" is not licensed: confounding by flow type, company, time, value band. Before/after comparisons across norm versions (1.6) show change, not effect; a difference-in-differences with control slices is a v2 design and is labelled quasi-experimental.
- **Silent norm learning.** Fitting thresholds to the data and applying them makes the norm circular; proposals only, with a chosen reference and sign-off (1.4).
- **p-values as the headline over thousands of slices.** Effect sizes in native units, intervals, support and stability badges instead; tests only where a null is meaningful (1.6, 1.8), with multiplicity stated.
- **Predictive process monitoring (next-activity / remaining-time deep models), trace embeddings, clustering on embeddings.** Heavy, opaque, unrelated to the descriptive contract; 1.10 covers the one operational question with an empirical survival curve.
- **Numbers computed by the LLM or re-implemented in the app.** Every displayed figure comes from `wise` or `wise_analytics`; the assistant reads result records (Sec. 4) and cites them.
- **Exposure-weighted PI without the exposure checks of 1.3.** Unit mixing or zero exposure silently reorders the backlog.

## 3. Feature store and pipeline
- **Single source of truth.** Per-case features are `EventLog.cases` (counts, `first_ts/last_ts`, attributes, exposure) plus derived attributes from the norm's `derived_attributes` recipes (`EventLog.derive`). Analytics never recompute a primitive: raw signals for graded constraints come from the same functions scoring uses (`log.first_after`, `log.total`, `log.count_scoped`), exposed as `raw_signals(log, norm)` (cases × constraints, native units). Proposal to upstream a `signal_matrix` into `wise-lib` later so the app carries no constraint semantics of its own. New case features an analytic needs (hand-off count, period key, subgroup indicator) are added *as recipes* in the norm, not as app code, so they are versioned with the fingerprint.
- **Artefacts and cache.** Content-addressed cache keyed by `(log_fingerprint, norm.fingerprint(), wise.__version__, scoring mode)`, where `log_fingerprint` hashes the source file bytes, column mapping, window and case-attribute list. Stored as Parquet/Arrow (float32 for `V`, `S`, `Δ`, `π`; categorical slice keys): BPIC'19 `V` is 251 734 × 29 ≈ 29 MB. Backlogs and analytic results are cached under an additional key of their parameters (`by`, view, γ, baseline, B, seed, grid). Cache lives in the workspace folder next to the norm versions; nothing leaves the machine.
- **Incremental per period.** Constraints are per-case, so `V` rows are independent: a new extract scores only new/changed cases (by case id + trace hash) and appends. What is *not* incremental and is recomputed each time: `μ̄` and backlogs (cheap), censoring end / observation window, and population-dependent recipes (`quantile_scale`) — mark such recipes and warn that their values shift with the population.
- **Compute budget (laptop, BPIC'19 scale).** Scoring 2 s; `π`, contrasts, headroom: groupby operations < 1 s; bootstrap B = 200 via weight matrices in chunks: ≈ 5–10 s; threshold envelope (29 constraints × 2 factors) via column re-evaluation + matrix product: < 10 s; norm what-if: 2 s per norm; subgroup search (10 attributes, depth 3, beam 20): 10–30 s; period series: one scoring pass plus per-period aggregation. Rule: interactive analytics < 5 s, otherwise a background job with progress and a cached result; memory ceiling 2 GB; every long job is cancellable and resumable from cache. Parallelism: constraint columns and bootstrap chunks are embarrassingly parallel (joblib/threads on numpy).

## 4. Explainability and presentation standards
- **Every result is a record with five parts:** claim in norm vocabulary (slice, layer, constraint names, view), evidence (n, means, deltas in native units), uncertainty (interval, stability badge), caveats (readiness flags for the slice, applicability differences), provenance (record id). Template (illustrative numbers): "Slice **A × Packaging** ranks #1 under **Automation** (90 % rank range 1–1, stable). Its stabilised gap of 0.0087 is carried by **L3 handovers & ageing** (0.0061) — median IR→CI lag 83 d vs 55 d elsewhere, whole distribution shifted. Readiness: no censoring, no replication. Headroom under the norm: meeting `c_l3_inv_to_cl` fully would remove at most 70 % of this PI."
- **Vocabulary.** Allowed: carries, concentrates, is associated with, differs by, headroom under the norm, action hypothesis. Forbidden in generated text: causes, because, will improve, predicts (except 1.10, "expected under the base rate"), root cause (unless quoting the analyst's own hypothesis). Numbers appear with their support (`n`) and unit every time.
- **Uncertainty display.** Intervals and rank ranges, not p-values; three badges (stable / fragile / insufficient support) with the rule shown on hover; sensitivity envelopes as "top-k under 6/6 settings"; control charts show limits and the expected false-alarm count.
- **Charts (one per analytic, reused everywhere).** Backlog strip with intervals; volume–gap scatter with PI iso-lines and whiskers (Fig. 6); gap waterfall by constraint (sums to the gap); ECDF overlay slice vs rest (Fig. 7); headroom bars; small-multiple control charts; co-occurrence heatmap with structural pairs greyed; signature/vendor Pareto (Fig. 9). Every chart has the same numbers in a table view and an export.
- **Audit trail.** One JSON record per analytic run: analytic name and version, `wise` version, log fingerprint, norm fingerprint and version label, view, `by`, γ, baseline, volume, parameters (seed, B, grid, thresholds), input record ids, output hash, runtime, warnings, and the readiness status at the time. Records are append-only and reproducible bit-for-bit. The assistant's messages must cite record ids; a message that states a number without a record is rejected by the app before display. Norm changes accepted from proposals (1.4) reference the proposal record.

## 5. Evaluation
- **Synthetic generator (`wise_analytics.synthetic`).** P2P-like logs with flow types, companies × spend areas × vendors, lognormal lags, amount mismatches, fragmentation counts, cancellations, resources, exposure; a seeded scenario file plants: hotspot slices with (i) lag shift ×k, (ii) missing-activity rate, (iii) fragmentation, (iv) attribute conjunctions not aligned with slice keys, (v) a step change at period `t*`; and artefacts: replicated header events, sentinel dates, right-truncated window, precision mix. Ground truth is stored with the log.
- **Metrics per analytic.** 1.1 interval coverage, badge calibration (share of *stable* slices that stay top-k on a fresh draw); 1.2 driver precision (top constraint = planted), shift/tail agreement; 1.3 artefact recall/precision; 1.4 threshold recovery error, applicability recovery; 1.5 identities; 1.6 detection delay, false-alarm rate; 1.7 signature recovery; 1.8 subgroup precision/recall, null false-discovery rate; 1.10 Brier score and calibration by backtest. Report all on a fixed scenario set in CI.
- **BPIC'19 as regression fixture.** Golden numbers from the paper: mean scores 0.819/0.817/0.871/0.844, applicability density 76.2 %, Compliance backlog 2.3 %/9.2 % of documents for 80 %/95 % of stable PI, top-20 overlap 0.481 (Fin–Compl) and 0.081 (Fin–Aut), Packaging 109 199 cases / gap 0.0087 / PI 945.7, IR→CI median 83 vs 55 d, 22 of 266 vendors for 80 % of Packaging penalty mass, Logistics replicated share 72.5 %, 0 % censoring in focus slices. Analytics outputs on BPIC'19 with fixed seeds are snapshotted and diffed with tolerances.
- **Property tests (hypothesis-style).** Exact decompositions (1.2, 1.5, 1.7); monotonicity (raising δ never increases ν; adding cases to a slice never changes other slices' means); invariance (relabelling slices, permuting cases, period concatenation equals full-log scoring); bootstrap interval contains the point estimate; what-if with identity norm reproduces the backlog.

## 6. Interfaces
Package `wise_analytics` inside `wise-workbench/packages/` (kept out of `wise-lib`, which stays numpy/pandas-only and method-pure). Every function takes `wise` objects and returns a frozen dataclass with `.table` (DataFrame), `.summary` (dict), `.record` (provenance) and `.readings` (list of template sentences).

**Dependencies.** Required: `numpy`, `pandas`, `pyarrow` (cache), `wise-pm`. Optional extras: `scipy` (k-medoids/hierarchical clustering, Mann–Whitney; already an extra of `wise-pm`), `scikit-learn` (only the shallow tree in 1.8 and nothing else — allowed because it is a mature, offline-installable package, but no analytic may *require* it), `joblib` (parallel chunks). Rejected: `lifelines` (the mature-cohort estimator and a 30-line Kaplan–Meier suffice), `statsmodels` (Wilson/Newcombe intervals, CUSUM and bootstrap are a few lines; keeps the on-prem install small), `shap`, gradient-boosting and deep-learning stacks (Sec. 2). `ollama` is a dependency of the assistant, never of `wise_analytics`.

## 7. Roadmap
- **MVP (4 items).** (1) `bootstrap_backlog` + stability badges on the backlog and the volume–gap scatter; (2) `contrast_slice` + `raw_signals` with the gap waterfall and ECDF overlay; (3) `readiness` gate wired to the backlog and to the assistant's guardrail; (4) `headroom` per driver. Plus the provenance `record` decorator from day one and the property tests of Sec. 5.
- **v1.** `period_backlogs`/`control_signals`; norm aids (`suggest_thresholds`, `suggest_applicability`, `constraint_health`, `cooccurrence`) in the norm editor; `sensitivity_envelope`; `whatif_norm`/`whatif_weights`; `violation_signatures`; `discover_subgroups` with holdout; the synthetic generator and the CI evaluation harness; BPIC'19 golden snapshots.
- **Later.** `open_case_risk` with backtesting; `handoffs`; quasi-experimental before/after with control slices (difference-in-differences on `μ_{s,t}`, labelled as such); upstreaming `signal_matrix` and the `handoffs` recipe into `wise-lib`; object-centric slices once the library supports them.
